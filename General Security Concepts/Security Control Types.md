In the previous section I mentioned there are 4 major categories of security controls, which tell us *what a control is made of*. Now we look at the control **types**, which tell us *what the control actually does*. There are 6 types, namely:
1. [[Preventive]]: Blocks access before anything happens
2. [[Deterrent]]: Discourages an attacker from even trying
3. [[Detective]]: Identifies and logs an intrusion attempt
4. [[Corrective]]: Fixes things after an event has been detected
5. [[Compensating]]: Steps in when the main control cannot be used
6. [[Directive]]: Tells people what they should do, without enforcing it

The easiest way for me to remember these is with a building. ==🟢ex. A locked door is preventive, a "no entry" warning sign is deterrent, a CCTV camera is detective, restoring the stolen equipment from insurance or backup is corrective, a backup generator is compensating and a policy that says "lock the door when you leave" is directive.==

Any single control belongs to a category and has a type. For example, a firewall is a [[Technical Controls]] category control and it is also a preventive type control. A control can also fit into more than one type, a security guard can be preventive, deterrent and detective all at the same time. In the exam you will typically be asked to pick the *best* fit based on the scenario they give.
