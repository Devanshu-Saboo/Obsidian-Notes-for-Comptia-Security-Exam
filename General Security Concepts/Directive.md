Directive control types are implemented when you wish to use weaker security controls, wherein you direct people to take an extra security step but it is not enforced in the slightest.

A good example of this would be a sign stating "authorized personnel only". It states that only authorized people are allowed, but without any security guards or other physical controls to actually prevent it, anyone may enter. ==🟢ex. Security policies, Standard Operating Procedures, Compliance rules, Posted warning signs, Training that tells employees what to do==

These rely on people choosing to follow the rules, which is why they are the weakest type. They are still important because they set the expectation and give the organization a reason to take action against someone who ignores it. This is the type that most [[Managerial Controls]] fall under.

The easiest way to remember the difference between this and [[Preventive]] is "can the control stop you?". A directive says "please do not", a preventive says "you cannot".
