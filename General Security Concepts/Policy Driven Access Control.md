After all this information is collected and the scope is reduced, an organization can implement policies that decide which types of authentication controls should be used for a particular user to successfully authenticate themselves and gain further access into the network.

For example, an employee logging in on a company laptop from inside the office might only need their password, whereas the same employee logging in from an unknown phone in another country would need a password and an OTP, and may only be given access to a limited set of resources.

So in short, [[Adaptive Identity]] gathers the information, [[Threat Scope Reduction]] limits the ways in, and the policy decides what the user has to do to get through. These policies are made in the [[Control Plane]].
