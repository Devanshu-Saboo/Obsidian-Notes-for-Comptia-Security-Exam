Most companies already have a change approval process in place and use it constantly to make changes in the organization.

This is a formal process for making changes because it helps avoid downtime and mistakes, ensuring the network is unaffected and business may operate at full capacity even once the changes are implemented.

There are various steps to this process such as:
1. ==🟠Completing the request forms==: The change is formally written down so there is a record of it
2. ==🟠Determining the purpose of the change==: Why are we doing this at all?
3. ==🟠Identifying the scope of the change==: Exactly what is and is not included
4. ==🟠Scheduling the date and time for the change==: Done so it causes the least disruption to the business
5. ==🟠Determining affected systems and their impact==: Who and what will feel this change
6. ==🟠Analyzing the risk associated with the change==: What could go wrong
7. ==🟠Getting approval from the change control board==: A group that reviews the request and either approves or denies it
8. ==🟠Getting end-user acceptance==: Confirming that the system is working fine on their end after the change
