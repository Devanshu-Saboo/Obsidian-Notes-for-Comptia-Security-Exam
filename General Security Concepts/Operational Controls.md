Operational controls are controls that are implemented by utilizing people instead of systems. These are the day to day security tasks that are carried out by the staff of the organization to follow the policies that the [[Managerial Controls]] have laid out. ==🟢ex. Security Guards, Security Awareness Training==

These types of controls are not technical in nature and are not implemented in systems. Instead, they use people to reduce and minimize the risk of a malicious attacker getting access to data. Some other examples are:
1. ==🟠Security Guards==: People who watch over entrances and check that only authorized people go through
2. ==🟠Awareness Training==: Teaching employees to spot things like phishing emails so they do not become the weakest link
3. ==🟠Day to day procedures==: Staff actually following the documented steps, such as reviewing access logs or signing visitors in

The reason these are important is that the best technical control in the world can still be beaten by a human making a mistake, for example an employee clicking on a link in a phishing email. Training the people is how we reduce that risk.

One thing that can get confusing is how this differs from [[Physical Controls]]. A security guard is a *person* doing the work so it is operational, whereas the lock on the door that the guard is standing next to is a physical object so it is physical.
