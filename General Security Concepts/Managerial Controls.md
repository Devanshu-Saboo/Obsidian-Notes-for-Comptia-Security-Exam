Managerial controls, also known as administrative controls, are the controls that come from the planning and management side of security. Instead of stopping an attacker directly, these set the rules and the direction that every other control follows. ==🟢ex. Security Policies, Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), Risk Assessments, Official Security Procedure==

I like to think of these as the rulebook of the organization. A firewall will not know what to block unless somebody has first decided what should be blocked, and that decision is made here. Some of the common managerial controls are:
1. ==🟠Security Policies==: Written rules on how the organization expects data and systems to be handled
2. ==🟠Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)==: Step by step documented processes for carrying out a task the same way every time. The change approval process we saw in [[Change Approval Process]] is a good example of this.
3. ==🟠Risk Assessments==: Identifying what could go wrong, how likely it is and how bad it would be, so we can spend effort where it matters most

It is necessary to ensure best practices in security, which I will further delve into later in the notes. By maintaining best practices we can minimize the risk and the damage done to the organization by any potential malicious attacker.

Managerial controls are usually the ones that start things off. The managerial side writes the policy, the [[Technical Controls]] and [[Operational Controls]] are then used to put that policy into practice.

> [!joke] If nobody writes the rules down, then technically nobody broke them.
