Technical controls, also known as logical controls, are controls that are implemented using systems. This is basically the technology that sits on our devices and networks and does the work of protecting our data without a person having to step in each time. ==🟢ex. Firewall, Anti-Virus, Operating System security settings==

These types of controls are used to secure data against online threats and attacks that come in through the systems and the network. Some other common examples of technical controls are:
1. ==🟠Firewalls==: Allow or block traffic going in and out of the network based on rules
2. ==🟠Anti-Virus / Anti-Malware==: Scan for and remove malicious software on a device
3. ==🟠IDS / IPS==: Systems that watch the network for suspicious activity and either alert about it or block it
4. ==🟠Access Control Lists==: Define who or what is allowed to access a particular resource
5. ==🟠Encryption==: Makes data unreadable to anyone who does not have the key, as we saw in [[Encrypting Data]]

An important thing to keep in mind is that the *category* of a control does not tell us what it does, that is where the control types come in. A firewall is a technical control, but it can also be a [[Preventive]] control since it blocks traffic, while the logs it produces would be a [[Detective]] control. We will see this properly in [[Security Control Types]].

Another thing that confuses people is where policies fit in. A rule such as "all laptops must have a firewall turned on" is a policy, which is a [[Managerial Controls]] thing, but the firewall that is actually turned on is the technical control.
