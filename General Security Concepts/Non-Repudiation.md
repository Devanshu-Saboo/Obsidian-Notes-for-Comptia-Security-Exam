Non-repudiation is simply proof that a 3rd party can use to verify that data you have sent really did originate from you, so you cannot later deny sending it. "Repudiate" basically means to deny, so non-repudiation means you cannot deny it.

A real life version of this is signing a contract. Your signature, together with the fact that the contract has not been altered since, means you cannot later claim you never agreed to it. ==🟢ex. An email signed digitally, A signed software update from a vendor==

It can be done in two ways:
1. [[Proof of Integrity]]: Proving the data was not changed
2. [[Proof of Origin]]: Proving who the data came from

Only when both are true can we have non-repudiation, since we need to know *who* sent it and that *what we received* is exactly what they sent. This is why it sits right next to [[Integrity]] but is its own concept.
